# Power Writing Playbook

*Master reference from Shaan Puri's 6-session Power Writing Course (shaanpuri.com)*

---

## The 7 Power Writing Hacks

1. **Write at 5th–8th grade reading level.** Simple words beat fancy words (30x more readers).
2. **Kill adverbs.** Cut anything ending in "-ly." Pick a stronger verb instead.
3. **Subject first.** "The boy hit the ball" — not "The ball was hit by the boy."
4. **Visual language.** "Build a huge wall" beats "improve national border security." Make people see it.
5. **Rhymes and alliteration.** They make ideas sticky. Not childish — persuasive.
6. **Keep it personal.** Use "I" or "you" — never "we" or "one."
7. **Spend 20%+ of time on the first line, last line, and formatting.**

**BONUS:** Write like you talk. Say it out loud first, then write what sounded good.

---

## Core Principles

- **Clear writing = clear thinking.** If you can't write it clearly, you don't understand it yet.
- **Start with the reaction you want.** Not what you want to say — what you want them to feel or do.
- **"It's ok if they hate it. It's ok if they love it. As long as they don't ignore it."**
- **People don't care about you. They care about themselves.** Every sentence should answer: "What's in it for me?"
- **Action comes from emotion.** 95% of decision-making is subconscious/emotional (Harvard Business School).
- **Your audience is "Debbie at her desk" — not a literary critic.** Write for real humans skimming on their phones.
- **4 readers > 40,000 readers — if they're the right 4.**
- **Go from Complicated to Powerful — not just Simple.** Simplicity without force is bland.

## Session 1: Cold Emails

### The Goal

Get a 1-word reply. "Yes." "Interested." "Sure." That's a win.

### Formula (AIDA Variation)

| Step | Purpose | How |
|------|---------|-----|
| **Attention** | Subject line creates a curiosity gap | Not too vague, not too specific |
| **Personal Touch** | Prove it's not mass spam | Reference something specific about them |
| **Offer & Benefits** | Be a giver, not a beggar | "I looked at your onboarding — here are 3 improvements" |
| **Credibility** | Not be on the bottom rung | Brief proof you're worth their time |

### The Scooter Braun Law

Make it easy to say Yes, No, or "cool, Marie will schedule."

### Key Rules

- Keep it short — the longer it is, the crazier you seem
- Be a giver, not an asker
- **80% of the magic is in the follow-up**
- Auto-follow-up at 1, 3, and 5 days
- Be politely persistent — bump with new value, not "just checking in"

### Follow-Up Examples

- "Wanted to give this a bump. You guys are my top choice, so you can't blame me for swinging for the fences, can you?"
- "Since that first email we've had a ton of progress. X Y & Z have come on board. Would love to have you on."
- "Did you have a chance to look at this? Slide 3 is the main thing that should get your attention."

## Session 2: Power Writing Habits + Pitching Yourself

### The Practice System

**Copywork:** Re-write great writing word-for-word. Not summarizing — re-writing.

- Hunter S. Thompson retyped The Great Gatsby
- Ben Franklin rewrote The Spectator from memory

**Copywork Sources:**

| Style | Source |
|-------|--------|
| Short business writing | "The Day You Became A Better Writer" (Scott Adams), Frank Slootman's memo, "We Don't Sell Saddles Here" (Slack) |
| Charming / charismatic | The Boron Letters (Gary Halbert), Sam Parr |
| Thoughtful / philosophical | Naval's "How to Get Rich Without Getting Lucky", Paul Graham's essays |

**Swipe File:** A running collection organized by category:

- Openers — first lines that hooked you
- Closers — endings that stuck
- Transition words
- Phrases / analogies
- Diagrams — visual explanations that worked
- Hero images
- Great ads

**Tools:** Hemingway App (writing tips as you type), HypeFury (Twitter threads)

### StoryBrand Formula (Pitching Anything)

Use for company pitches, product descriptions, bios, about pages, landing pages.

**3 Steps:**

1. **Start with the problem:** "Most [people / job title] struggle with _____"
2. **Simple solution:** "That's why we created _____"
3. **Happy ending:** Paint the opposite of the problem

**Tips:**

- Use "most people" or "most [job title]" — inclusive, non-judgmental
- Name the customer, not your company
- Focus on what they *feel*, not what you judge about them
- Secret: go from Complicated to Powerful, not just Simple

### Example (Weak → Strong)

**Weak:** "Learn the fundamental techniques and psychology of effective power writing"

**Strong:** "Most people have ideas in their head, but feel stressed trying to write them out clearly. This is a 10 day crash course in business writing. So your writing turns into a lean mean crisp writing machine."

## Session 3: Viral Writing

### Two Parts: IDEAS + PACKAGING

### Finding Ideas

**Content Creator Types:**
- **Generous Expert** — you know things and share freely
- **Curious Beginner** — you're learning in public, taking people along
- Stunt Devils, Analysts with Attitude, Meme Lords

**Content Generator Matrix:**

Cross any row with any column:

|  | Job | Investment | Skill | Thing Learned | Advice | Book | Side Hustle |
|--|-----|-----------|-------|--------------|--------|------|-------------|
| **First** | | | | | | | |
| **Last** | | | | | | | |
| **Best** | | | | | | | |
| **Worst** | | | | | | | |
| **Weirdest** | | | | | | | |

**Mining for Gold:** Reddit threads, niche forums, internet deep dives. Remix and repackage for your audience. Original framing > original research.

### The Secret to Going Viral

**Don't start with:** "What do I want to say?"

**Start with:** "What reaction do I want the reader to have?"

### 8 Core Emotions to Target

Power writers want action. Action comes from emotion. Target these emotions in your writing to drive shares, clicks, and engagement.

### The Hook (Slippery Slope)

- Goal of sentence 1 = get them to read sentence 2
- Goal of sentence 2 = get them to read sentence 3
- Creates a slippery slope — readers beg for the payoff

### Posting Strategy

Don't tweet 10x a day. **5 quality threads, 1x per month = better growth.**

### Hook Examples

- "I haven't talked about this publicly ever... but finally, I'm ready."
- "I was wrong about X. I tried it & the results were nutty."
- Big bold scroll-stopping images beat boring graphs

## Session 4: Headlines

### The Core Problem

Writers spend 5% on the headline. Readers spend 80% on the headline. **Power writers spend at least 30% of time on the headline.**

### The Curiosity Gap

| Type | Example | Result |
|------|---------|--------|
| Too vague | "Partnership Opportunity" | Nobody clicks |
| Too specific | Gives away the answer | No reason to click |
| Just right | "Jamie — want free luggage for life?" | Personalized + curious |

### Headline Rules

- Simple words beat fancy words (30x more readers)
- Clear storyline beats brainstorm-style
- Promise something juicy
- Ask for opinion instead of selling ("I need your opinion — is this the cutest outfit or what?" = 32% more clicks)

### The 25 Headline Exercise (from Upworthy)

1. Write 25 variations of your headline
2. First 10 = obvious
3. Next 10 = creative
4. Last 5 = your winner
5. Takes 5–10 minutes with templates

### Headline Template Examples

- "Here's what ___ and ___ have in common"
- "The single mistake that ___"
- "If you don't know the ___ theory, you're in trouble"
- "The little-known secret behind ___"
- "Dear [group]. Send this to your [role]. They need to read it."
- "I learned this the hard way. What I got wrong at ___"

### Email Subject Line Examples

**Boring:** "New Styles For Summer!"
**Better:** "New Stripes & Stars Style for 4th of July!"
**Best:** "i need your opinion - is this the cutest 4th of july outfit or what?!" (32% more clicks)

## Session 5: Landing Pages

### How the Brain Reads a Landing Page

1. "What's in it for me?" — Hook and value prop
2. "Is this legit?" — Trust and proof
3. "How do I get it?" — Clear CTA
4. "What's in it for me again?" — Reinforce before they click

### Your Job

Get it. Believe it. Take action.

### 5 Boosters

#### Boost #1 — Level-Up (Show How You Help)

Don't say what the product is. Show how it helps them.

**Two frames:**
- **Stop the Struggle:** Show the pain they currently feel
- **The Happy Ending:** Show what life looks like after

#### Boost #2 — Trust

Build belief with:
- Logos of companies you work with
- Before/After pics and case studies
- Reviews and star ratings
- Testimonials and quotes
- Social media love (screenshots)
- Help desk + lock icons
- Links to stalk more (about page, social)

#### Boost #3 — "But" Killer (Side-Step Hesitation)

Combine your benefit with the objection killer:

| Benefit | The "But" | Both |
|---------|-----------|------|
| Learn Spanish | Takes too long | Learn Spanish in just 10 minutes a day |
| Boost testosterone | Is it safe? | Boost T-levels without injections, supervised by doctors |

#### Boost #4 — Action (Simplify the Decision)

- **One CTA, not multiple.** Don't confuse them.
- Remove their concern right next to the button
- Point to value, don't push to action
  - Bad: "Sign up" / "Submit"
  - Good: "Start my free trial" / "Get my report"

#### Boost #5 — Personal

- Share your own story with the problem
- Have an attitude — generic is forgettable
- Read it out loud and kill the cringey jargon

**Example (boring → personal):**
- Boring: "I take great photos and videos of your wedding."
- Personal: "I screwed up at my wedding. My uncle Eddy said he could take pics for free... Big mistake. He got drunk. That's why I created Your Big Day Photography."

### Landing Page Health Check

- [ ] Do I get it?
- [ ] Do I believe it?
- [ ] Do I wanna do it?

### Traps to Avoid

- Don't copy big brands — Nike has brand awareness, you don't
- Avoid generic stock images — nobody gets turned on by that

## Session 6: Writing Like Jeff Bezos

### The WHAT → WHY → SO WHAT Framework

Use for investor updates, internal memos, status reports, team updates.

| Section | Question | Example |
|---------|----------|---------|
| **WHAT** | What happened? How's it going? | "This was an awesome month. Revenue was XX, up Y%." |
| **WHY** | Why did it happen? | "Sales went up because social media growth led to more customers." |
| **SO WHAT** | What are we doing about it? | "Let's double down on social. Here's my ask of you..." |

### Common Mistakes

- Forgetting what the reader cares about
- Data dumping without a narrative
- Missing a clear ask
- Only sharing good news

### The Internal Newsletter Hack

Write a short weekly email to a curated list of key people.

**Format:** 1 photo, 2 thoughts, 3 links

**How to start:**
1. Every Sunday, write an email
2. Create a simple format (1-2-3 works great)
3. Opt-in your favorite people in the company
4. After 3 sends, say "reply if you wanna stay on"
5. Skip the corporate ladder

**Why it works:**
- Live in powerful people's heads rent-free
- Create room for serendipity
- Forces you to step back from the weeds weekly
- Builds trust through consistency

**Key insight:** "4 readers > 40,000 readers" — if they're the right 4

## Quick-Reference Checklist

Use before publishing anything:

- [ ] 5th–8th grade reading level?
- [ ] Adverbs killed?
- [ ] Active voice (subject first)?
- [ ] Can the reader *see* what I'm describing?
- [ ] 30%+ time spent on headline / first line?
- [ ] First line makes them want to read the second?
- [ ] Writing to "you" — one person — not a crowd?
- [ ] Answers "What's in it for me?" in the first few seconds?
- [ ] One clear action I want them to take?
- [ ] Read it out loud? Sounds like a real person?
- [ ] Would I forward this to a friend?
