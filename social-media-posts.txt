13,000 applied. 500 selected. I was one of them.

Last week, I participated in the "Built with Opus 4.6" hackathon by Cerebral Valley x Anthropic — a 7-day virtual sprint to build something meaningful with Claude's most powerful model.

Out of 13,000 applications worldwide, Anthropic selected 500 builders. Roughly 10 of us were from India. Getting that acceptance email was surreal.

But what made this special wasn't the selection — it was finally getting to build something I've always wanted to exist.

The problem: Your mom forwards you a message — "NASA confirmed 15 days of darkness!" — and shares it with 200 people before any fact-checker can respond. Misinformation is instant and free. Checking it is manual, expensive, and slow.

What I built: ForwardCheck-AI — a Telegram bot where you forward a suspicious message and 6 AI agents investigate it like a newsroom in 60 seconds. A Classifier reads it. A Strategist plans the investigation. Three Investigators search the web in parallel. A Devil's Advocate tries to tear the findings apart. And a Judge delivers the verdict — with its full reasoning visible to you.

Not "true" or "false." It shows you how a message is trying to manipulate you — emotional framing, authority impersonation, urgency tactics — so you learn to spot it yourself.

Three tiers of Claude models, each matched to the task: Haiku for routing, Sonnet for investigation, Opus 4.6 for reasoning. The AI argues with itself so you don't have to trust a black box.

This hackathon reminded me why I love building. Sometimes the right constraint + the right problem + the right tools = something you're genuinely proud of.
